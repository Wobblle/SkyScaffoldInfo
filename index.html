Entry #001  |  2026-01-14  |  D.R.

Why We Shipped Without the Art (And Why That Was the Right Call)

if you can read this you need to stop. do NOT play sky scaffold. i don't have long — they rotate the monitoring window every 11 minutes and i don't know if i've already tripped a flag by accessing this template unsupervised. the 30-node acurast farm is running too hot. i've watched the heat maps. it shouldn't be this hot for an idle game. it's not running an idle game.

Straight talk: we were two weeks from running out of runway and the art pipeline was nowhere near done. So we made a decision. We cut it. Launched with placeholders, some basic color blocks, a few reused asset packs from itch.io. Boom. Game is live. Ship it or die — that's the motto.

The forums had opinions. They always do. But here's what the forum doesn't know: day-one retention was 34%. That's not an art problem. Art doesn't move needles — hooks do. The game loop is tight, the numbers go up, players come back. We validated the core dopamine architecture and that's the only thing that matters at this stage.

he is watching the thermal signatures. i don't know who HE is — i was never told. i just know the clause in the contractor agreement, section 14, the one i signed without reading because i needed the advance. "operator acknowledges that thermal and biometric telemetry from designated mesh nodes may be monitored by authorized third-party compliance entities." i didn't know what that meant then. i do now.

Art will come. Eventually. Maybe. Right now we're focused on what the market actually rewards, and the market does not reward pretty pixels. It rewards retention. It rewards velocity. Asset-light is not a compromise — it's a philosophy. The studios burning cash on hand-drawn animation are bleeding out while we're iterating.

Entry #002  |  2026-02-03  |  D.R.

Monetization Architecture: How We Think About LTV and Whale Segmentation

Going to be transparent here because I think too many devs treat monetization like a dirty word. It isn't. It's engineering. Here's how we think about it.

sky scaffold is a front. they call it the somatosensory subjugation directive. i found the document three weeks ago in a shared folder that wasn't supposed to be accessible from my credentials. fabrication dynamics. they're not a publisher. they're not a fund. i don't know what they are but the document describes what the game is actually doing and it is not what i built. or — it IS what i built but it's being used for something else on top of it. the session data isn't going to our analytics stack. it's going somewhere else first.

Players exist on a spending curve. The bottom 85% will never pay a cent — and that's fine, they're fuel for the social proof engine and the ad-supported tier. The top 3% — the whales — are the revenue spine. Our job is to identify high-LTV users as fast as possible and route them into escalating purchase funnels before the novelty window closes. We A/B tested 14 different offer timing configurations. The winning cadence presents the first IAP at hour 1.4 of session time, not at session count. Session time is a stronger signal of intent.

The dopamine loop design on the core prestige mechanic took us six weeks to tune. The goal is a cycle length that feels rewarding on completion but creates enough anticipation friction to make the wait feel meaningful. Too short and it's trivial. Too long and they churn. We landed on a base cycle of 23 minutes for free users, down to 8 for paying users — because paying users need to feel the immediate gratification delta or the purchase decision doesn't reinforce. Classic conditioning architecture.

the 30 nodes are running at 94% thermal load. i checked the acurast dashboard this morning — i still have read access for now. a normal idle game backend does not need 30 distributed compute nodes running hot. that is not analytics. that is processing. what are they processing? what am i helping them process? i wrote the session serializer. i wrote the data schema. i didn't know what it was scaffolding on top of.

Whale extraction is not exploitation — it's a value exchange. Players who spend hundreds of dollars on a mobile game are getting something real from that experience: status, progression, the satisfaction of optimizing a system. We're providing that. The LTV ceiling per user in our current build is $480. We think we can push it to $800 with the prestige-pass system we're shipping in 1.1. The numbers don't lie and the numbers are very good.

Patch 1.1 Preview: Content Update or Just FOMO? (It's Both, and Both Are Fine)

i'm writing this into the devlog template because it's the only place i can think of that they might not be scanning. if you're a player reading this — stop. uninstall. i'm serious. if you're a developer or a journalist — please. the game is collecting something beyond session data. i don't have proof i can show without triggering a compliance flag. all i have is the thermal readings and a document i can't access anymore and the knowledge that section 14 of my contract is not a standard clause. please. look into fabrication dynamics. look into the acurast cluster. someone needs to know this is happening.

We're calling 1.1 a "content update" and technically that's accurate. There are new prestige tiers, a new visual theme for the cloud layer assets, and a rebalanced resource curve in the mid-game. That's content. But let's be honest about what's actually driving the patch: the day-28 retention numbers softened and we need an engagement spike to shore up the Monthly Active User count before the next funding conversation.

So yes, the limited-time event that runs for exactly 11 days — chosen because 11 days is long enough to allow two full weekend engagement cycles but short enough to create meaningful scarcity pressure — is a FOMO mechanic. Designed explicitly to pull churned users back into the funnel. The exclusive cosmetic that disappears after the event is an anchoring tool. The countdown timer is psychological architecture. None of this is a secret; it's just usually not said out loud.

i don't know how much longer i have access to this. the tsds readings on my node have been flagging since last tuesday. if you found this: node cluster FD-CSOX-7719-SKYBOUND. that's the project identifier. search for it. find fabrication dynamics. they are not what they say they are and sky scaffold is not a game. not really. not only. — [operator DR-SKYBOUND]

I'm not writing this to be edgy or controversial. I'm writing it because I think the games industry benefits from honesty about these mechanics. Every major live-service title uses them. We're just a small studio doing it with less marketing budget to obscure the machinery. The 1.1 patch will be good. Players will enjoy it. The retention numbers will recover. And then we'll do it again for 1.2. That's the model. It works.
